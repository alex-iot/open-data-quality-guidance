# Change log

## 0.1, draft for comment

Documents dated 12 November 2026.

- First draft of the guidance: ten principles, three levels, what a good dataset entry looks like, portal checks and maintenance rules.
- Three levels a dataset earns by evidence (Stated, Consistent, Kept consistent), from a starting point of Not stated. The check period for level 3 is a starting value and has not yet been tested in practice.
- Two kinds of rule: those that hold a publisher to its own statement, and those that come from an outside norm. The gates at levels 2 and 3 are all of the first kind.
- Level 1 asks for a title and description, a licence given as a link to its text, and an update frequency from the controlled vocabulary that applies. A licence that is stated but not open passes level 1 and carries a warning flag.
- Level 2 asks, among other things, that a date the entry gives for the data is not older than the content. Data marked "irregular" or "as needed" makes no promise about timing and must give such a date.
- The items the level 1 gates need must appear on the dataset's page and in the portal's feed, and say the same in both.
- An example scorecard for a dataset and for a portal, and thirteen typical failures, each with the gate it fails, if any.
- Legal references named by instrument; DCAT-AP references checked against DCAT-AP 3.0.1 and the annex for high-value datasets 3.0.0.
- Related work: the Open Data Institute's Open Data Certificates and its framework for AI-ready data, and ISO/IEC 25012, ISO/IEC 5259-2 and ISO 8000-8, placed against the guidance in "Why this guidance" and added to the references.
- Principles reordered so that the three that hold a publisher to its own statement come first: "Every copy says the same" is now principle 3 (previously 10), and principles 3 to 9 become 4 to 10.
- Includes the two companion documents: the rule reference (40 rules and ten maintenance rules, each with an identifier, basis, level and kind) and the worked example in DCAT-AP 3.0.1, with its three example records.
